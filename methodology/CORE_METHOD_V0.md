# CP-01 — Interest to Business Core Method v0 (manual, portable)

**Status:** draft for owner review. **MPE:** EXTEND_EXISTING. No software is required.
**Promise:** help a person decide which interest-backed opportunity is worth testing, not promise every interest can generate a business.

## Compact definitions
- **Interest:** a topic/activity a person willingly spends time or effort on. Not evidence of a market.
- **Observed need:** a person's desired progress or persistent obstacle in a specific circumstance.
- **Segment:** people sharing the relevant circumstance and decision ability, not just demographics.
- **Problem:** an observed gap with frequency, consequence and existing workarounds.
- **Value hypothesis:** a proposed improvement meaningful enough for someone to exchange money, time or access.
- **Evidence:** traceable observation of behaviour, payment, time, workflow or experiment; separate quotation from interpretation.
- **Pattern hypothesis:** recurrent mechanism across independent cases with conditions, alternative explanations and counterexamples.

## Seven steps — complete in a notebook
| Step | Question | Minimum artifact | Failure/stop signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Interest | Why do I know/care about this, what can I actually deliver? | 3 skills/experiences + constraints | no capability, access or interest to continue |
| 2 People | Whose specific situation might benefit? | one concrete segment + decision maker | cannot identify or reach relevant people |
| 3 Need | What do they already do, struggle with or pay for? | 3 concrete instances, sources and alternatives | only imagined or hypothetical needs |
| 4 Problem | What is the cost of status quo and why now? | frequency, impact, prior attempts, competing explanations | trivial harm / no urgency |
| 5 Value | What simplest improvement could help? | 2 alternatives, success measure, safety/privacy limits | no measurable improvement / harmful path |
| 6 Test | What cheap reversible action could disprove us? | hypothesis, test population, baseline, duration, stop rule | cannot execute ethically or observe outcome |
| 7 Decide | What happened and for whom? | result, hours/costs, confidence, exceptions, STOP/ITERATE/SCALE | weak result or disconfirming evidence ⇒ STOP/ITERATE |

## Evidence levels (not numeric certainty scores)
- E0 ASSUMPTION: unsupported intuition; cannot justify implementation.
- E1 SELF-OBSERVED: firsthand case with source and baseline, not proof of general market.
- E2 EXTERNAL-BEHAVIOUR: independent people report verifiable past events or show workarounds.
- E3 TEST-OUTCOME: a falsifiable intervention yields measured result against baseline.
- E4 COMMERCIAL: actual exchange/payment with known delivery costs, no presumption of scalable demand.

Each case must record observation, method, date, source reference, limitations, disconfirming evidence and consent/retention constraints.

## Decisions
STOP when harm, no repeated pain, no reachable buyer, weak evidence after a bounded test or unsustainable unit economics.
ITERATE when problem is evidenced but segment, offer or method is wrong or uncertain.
SCALE only after reproducible outcomes, commercial willingness, acceptable economics and explicit new MPE/owner gate. Scaling is not automatic.

## Pattern discipline
When two cases look alike, write a **pattern hypothesis**, not a universal law. Test at least one plausible confounder, one counterexample, and a boundary condition before using it across markets. Country law, language, purchasing power and sector can change execution even when the need is similar.
