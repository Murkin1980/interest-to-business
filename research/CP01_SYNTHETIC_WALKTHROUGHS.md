# CP-01 — synthetic walkthroughs and acceptance review

These are **fictional** examples to check whether a person can use the method, **not market evidence**.

## Example A — furniture quotations
1. Interest: owner understands kitchen manufacturing and estimating.
2. People: small workshops receiving custom-kitchen inquiries.
3. Need: hypothetical visitors want a fast, clear quote; workshop manually collects dimensions.
4. Problem hypothesis: delays can lose customers. E0 — no actual timing or conversion figures supplied.
5. Value options: clear intake checklist versus automatic calculator; prefer cheap checklist first.
6. Test plan: with permission, compare 10 consecutive inquiries using the checklist to a recorded comparable baseline; measure time-to-quote, mistakes and conversion, including lead quality. Predefine acceptable effort and stop conditions.
7. Decision: **NOT TESTED**. No claim of increased sales. Counterexample: quotes may be delayed due to complex designs rather than intake.

## Example B — creator's interest in urban gardening
1. Interest: a creator enjoys balcony gardening and has documented a few seasons.
2. People: apartment residents with limited light and gardening time.
3. Need hypothesis: uncertainty about which plants survive in low light.
4. Problem: no evidence of a costly enough problem or buying behaviour, E0.
5. Value options: free example guide vs. small paid planning session.
6. Test plan: discuss recent planting purchases and failures with five relevant people without leading questions; invite a small real pre-order if a repeated unmet need appears; record refusals and delivery time.
7. Decision: **NOT TESTED**. STOP if no repeated need or if readily available guides already suffice. Counterexample: people enjoy trial-and-error and don't want paid advice.
